Frozen Pipes in North Ridgeville: How to Thaw Them, Prevent Them, and Spot a Burst

If a faucet runs only a trickle or not at all in cold weather, a pipe is probably frozen. Open that faucet, find the frozen section, and warm it slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, never a flame. If water appears where it should not, shut off the main and call a plumber.

A frozen pipe is not yet a burst pipe, but ice can crack the pipe as it thaws. This guide covers the safe steps, the mistakes that cause damage, how to prevent a repeat, and what to do if the pipe has already failed.

A pipe is frozen right now: what to do in order

  1. 1Open the faucet on the frozen line, hot and cold if both are affected. Water needs somewhere to go as the ice melts.
  2. 2Find the frozen spot. Look for frost on the pipe, a section that feels colder, or a bulge. Common spots are along exterior walls and near basement windows.
  3. 3Locate the main shutoff before you start so you can close it quickly if the pipe leaks.
  4. 4Apply gentle heat. Use a hair dryer on low, an electric heating pad wrapped around the pipe, or towels soaked in warm water. Start nearest the faucet and work back toward the frozen part.
  5. 5Keep going until full flow returns. Then check along the pipe for drips or wet spots.

If you cannot find the frozen section or it is behind a wall, stop and call. Cutting into drywall blindly can turn a minor freeze into a bigger repair.

What not to do with a frozen pipe

A few instincts do more harm than good. Avoid these:

  • ✓Open flame: a torch or lighter can ignite framing or insulation and overheat the pipe.
  • ✓Boiling water poured on a pipe, which can crack a cold pipe or fitting through sudden temperature change.
  • ✓Space heaters left unattended on or near wet areas or flammable materials.
  • ✓Walking away while the faucet is open. Once the ice melts, a cracked section can spray.
  • ✓Ignoring a silent line. A frozen pipe that is not heated stays a risk until it thaws and may split first.

Heat applied slowly lets the ice melt from the faucet outward, which relieves pressure as it goes. Fast heat on a pipe that is already stressed can finish the job the ice started.

Where pipes freeze in northern Ohio houses

Northern Ohio has hard freezes, snow and thaw cycles, and the pipes that fail are rarely in the warm center of the house. They sit where cold air reaches them.

Shutoffs, permits and who to call in North Ridgeville

If you cannot find or turn your shutoff, call the City of North Ridgeville Public Utilities Division at (440) 490-2049, Monday to Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. They can answer questions about locating the meter and the water service shutoff. Do that before the first freeze, since a stuck valve is hard to deal with during an emergency.

After a burst, repairs that change the water supply need a permit under the city's rules, so ask the Building Division at (440) 490-2081 whether yours does. Permit rules vary by job, and the city also requires contractors working in it to register. Your plumber should know both.

Homes near North Olmsted and Sheffield Village have their own local offices and rules for these questions. The same hard freezes hit the whole region, but each town's permit process is its own.

Did it burst? Signs to look for after a thaw

Ice can crack a pipe without any sign until the thaw. Water then appears where the crack opened. After any freeze, check for these:

  • ✓A new wet spot on a ceiling, wall or floor that was dry before.
  • ✓A drop in pressure or a faucet that runs weak after thawing.
  • ✓Running water sounds when everything is off.
  • ✓A moving water meter with the house main open and all fixtures off.
  • ✓Warm or damp spots on a wall where the pipe runs.

If water is flowing, close the main and call the 24/7 line. Our burst pipe repair work covers cracked copper and other supply lines. If you suspect a slow leak and cannot see where, leak detection finds it before the damage spreads.

Close the main first, then open a low faucet and a high one to drain the system. Turn off power to any wet area before touching anything in it.

Stopping the next freeze before it starts

Most prevention is small and takes an afternoon. Do these before the first hard freeze:

  • ✓Disconnect garden hoses and shut off the interior valve to outdoor spigots, if one exists.
  • ✓Insulate exposed pipes in basements, garages and crawlspaces with foam sleeves.
  • ✓Seal air leaks around where pipes enter the house.
  • ✓Keep the heat on in vacant rooms and during travel, set to a steady comfortable level.
  • ✓Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze.
  • ✓Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. Moving water is slower to freeze.

Know where your main shutoff is before you need it. Test it once, so you know it turns.
