Water bubbling up in the yard, a meter that spins with every tap closed, or a house that suddenly lost pressure can all trace back to the buried pipe between the street and your foundation. The line is open 24/7. Close the main valve inside the house, keep people off soft, soggy ground, and call.

A leak under the yard that you cannot see

Water leaking from a pressurized buried pipe takes the easiest path: up to the surface, along a gravel bed, or toward a basement wall. The wet spot may sit far from the break, so the dig starts at the leak, not at the puddle.

The clues are a meter that moves with everything inside off, a lawn patch that stays soggy or green in dry weather, a dip in the ground, and a hiss near the meter. When the source is not obvious, leak detection with listening equipment narrows the search before any digging.

Low water pressure all through the house

Weak flow at every tap, hot and cold, points upstream of the fixtures: a partly closed main or meter valve, a worn pressure regulator, a leaking or corroded service line, or a blocked inlet screen.

A quick test helps. Compare an outdoor spigot with a tap upstairs. If both are weak, look at the main. If only some fixtures are weak, or rusty water comes from one run, galvanized pipe inside the walls is the likelier cause, and that is a question for repiping rather than the buried line.

Adding a new water line to a garage, addition or outbuilding

New lines branch off the service line or run on their own from the meter, and both need a trench and a shutoff you can reach. Construction water outlets need a code-approved backflow device in North Ridgeville, so take the plan to the Building Division first. Plumbing installation covers the work that continues inside the structure.

How the plumber finds the break and reaches it

  1. 1Prove it is the line. Close the main inside, watch the meter, and rule out a toilet or irrigation zone that mimics a leak.
  2. 2Trace and pinpoint. A pipe locator follows the route, then ground microphones and an isolated pressure test narrow the break to a small stretch.
  3. 3Mark utilities and dig. Buried gas, electric and phone lines are marked through the 811 service before anyone opens the ground.
  4. 4Repair or replace. A short repair joins new pipe with couplings. A longer failure is often better answered by a new line.

Afterward, the line is pressure-tested and the meter is watched with all fixtures off.

What decides the price of a water line repair

Digging is the unknown, which is why a fair price follows a look at the site. These factors change it.

A short trench in soft soil is a smaller job than a long run through compacted ground or roots.

Lawn, driveway, walkway and landscaping each cost something different to open and restore.

A spot repair is limited. A new line adds pipe, fittings and a pressure test for the whole length.

A replacement may need a permit and inspection, and an after-hours call is priced differently from a planned visit.

Insurance usually responds to sudden accidental damage inside the home, but a worn-out buried line is often treated as maintenance, and some policies offer separate service line coverage. For a real figure, call the line and describe where the water is showing up.

Signs your water service line is leaking or failing

  • ✓A water bill that jumps with no change in how the household uses water.
  • ✓Meter movement with every fixture off, including the ice maker and any irrigation system.
  • ✓A wet, sunken or unusually green patch between the meter and the house.
  • ✓A hissing sound near the meter box or where the pipe enters the foundation.
