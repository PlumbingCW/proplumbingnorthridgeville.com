Winter in North Olmsted: frozen pipes and what happens when they thaw

Northern Ohio gets hard freezes and snow. Water in a pipe expands when it freezes, and the damage often shows up only when it thaws and flows again. A pipe that was frozen quietly overnight can become a spray by afternoon.

  • ✓Know the signs: a faucet that gives only a trickle in cold weather, frost on an exposed pipe, or a pipe against an outside wall that feels icy.
  • ✓Open the faucet and leave it slightly running while you work on thawing, so melting water has somewhere to go.
  • ✓Warm gently with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use an open flame.
  • ✓Shut the main if you see a crack or hear water. Then call the line.

If a pipe has already split, see burst pipe repair. Pipes in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces freeze first, so keep those areas above cold and open cabinet doors on bitter nights.

Spring thaw and heavy rain: basements, sump pumps and sewer backups

As snow melts and spring rain arrives, the ground around a foundation soaks up water. Basements are common in this part of Ohio, and a sump pump is the first defense. If the pump fails during a storm, the pit overflows within minutes.

Test your pump before the wet season by pouring water into the pit and watching it start and empty. If it hesitates or makes noise, call for sump pump repair. When floor drains back up in a heavy storm, the cause may be a stressed sewer line, and basement waterproofing work on the plumbing side may help.

North Olmsted has its own utilities and permits, separate from North Ridgeville's. Call the North Olmsted offices for city-side questions, and call us when water is already in the basement. Stop using drains, keep out of the water near the panel, and describe how high it has come.

A postwar boom town: why so many North Olmsted houses are the same age

North Olmsted began as Lenox Township in 1823 and became a city in 1909. Then it exploded: 6,604 residents in 1950, 16,290 in 1960, and 34,861 in 1970. The peak came in 1980 at 36,480. The 2020 count was 32,442, with 14,390 housing units.

That boom means much of the housing is postwar, and many houses were built within the same few decades. The tie to plumbing is that systems installed at similar times wear out at similar times. Water heaters, supply valves and drain fittings in homes of that vintage are reaching the age where parts fail, and a failure on your street is a hint to check your own.

When one home needs water heater replacement, its neighbors are often not far behind. Replace on your schedule when the tank is old, not during a leak.

Summer storms and the water heater you forgot

Warm months bring thunderstorms and quick downpours. They also bring the time when homeowners finally notice a rust-colored puddle under the tank.

  • ✓Storm power outages can disturb pilot lights and thermostats. Check before assuming the tank has failed.
  • ✓Rust at the base of a tank means the shell is failing. See water heater repair to find out what is fixable.
  • ✓Lukewarm water with the power on points to a heating element or thermostat.
  • ✓Sump discharge that pools against the foundation sends water straight back to the wall.

Fall prep: the plumbing checklist before the first freeze

Autumn is the easiest time to prevent winter calls. A short walk-through catches the weak points.

  1. 1Disconnect hoses. Remove outdoor hoses and close the interior shutoff to outdoor spigots where there is one.
  2. 2Insulate exposed pipes. Wrap lines in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  3. 3Test the sump pump. Do it before leaves clog the discharge.
  4. 4Flush the water heater schedule. If your tank is old, ask a plumber whether to replace it before winter.
  5. 5Find the main shutoff. Make sure everyone in the house knows where it is.

Kitchens, bathrooms and the calls that do not follow the weather

Plenty of calls have nothing to do with the season. Garbage disposals jam, toilets run, faucets drip and kitchen drains slow down in July as often as in January. Those are good jobs for a same-day appointment when the schedule allows.

  • ✓A jammed disposal: switch it off, do not reach inside, and see garbage disposal repair.
  • ✓A toilet that runs or rocks: usually worn internal parts or a loose flange. See toilet repair.
  • ✓A slow kitchen drain: grease buildup, handled by drain cleaning.
  • ✓A new fixture or bathroom addition: that is a planned job, see plumbing installation.

If you are not sure which category your problem falls into, call the line and describe it. The dispatcher will help you decide whether it needs a plumber right now.

Tell the dispatcher the season and the weather too. A slow drain during a long rain, for example, can mean the sewer line is under stress, which changes what the plumber brings and how the visit goes.
