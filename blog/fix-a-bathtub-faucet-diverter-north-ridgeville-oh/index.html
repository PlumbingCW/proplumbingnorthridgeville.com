How to Fix a Bathtub Faucet Diverter

A bathtub diverter is the part that sends water to the spout or up to the shower head. When it wears out, you get water dribbling from the tub spout while the shower runs, or a weak shower, or no switch at all. The fix is usually a new diverter valve or a new spout, not the whole faucet.

First identify which kind you have, because the repair for a pull-up knob on the spout is different from a three-handle valve in the wall. Start with the table below.

Which kind of diverter is in your tub

Three common setups and where the diverter lives
SetupHow it worksWhere the fault is
Pull-up knob on the tub spoutLifting the knob blocks the spout and sends water upWorn seal inside the spout
Push-pull handle or button on the faucetA separate valve switches flowStem or cartridge in the wall
Three-handle faucetThe middle handle is the diverterIts stem, seat or washer
Single handle with a diverter leverLever or rod routes water within the valve bodyO-rings or diverter cartridge

A quick check: if the knob sits on top of the spout, you have a spout diverter, and the fix is often the easiest. Anything inside the wall takes more care and sometimes more tools.

Signs the diverter is wearing out

  • ✓Water keeps pouring from the spout when the shower is on. The seal no longer blocks the opening.
  • ✓The shower head is weak while the spout flows freely. Flow is leaking down before it can rise.
  • ✓The knob will not stay up and drops back to tub mode after a few seconds.
  • ✓A handle feels loose or gritty when turned. Mineral buildup or a worn stem is the likely cause.
  • ✓The spout drips after you turn off the water. A damaged seat inside the valve is leaking.

Mixing handle problems with diverter problems is common. If the water is the wrong temperature, that is a mixing valve or cartridge, not the diverter.

Replace a spout diverter in five steps

  1. 1Find the fastening. Look underneath the spout for a small set screw and hex hole. If there is none, the spout threads onto the pipe and turns off by hand.
  2. 2Remove the old spout. Loosen the screw with the right hex key or grip the spout body with a cloth-wrapped wrench and turn it counterclockwise.
  3. 3Clean the pipe stub. Remove old tape, putty or mineral crust so the new spout seals cleanly.
  4. 4Match the new spout. Measure the pipe length from the wall and note whether it is copper or threaded. Bring the old spout to the store.
  5. 5Fit and test. Install the new spout, snug the screw or turn it hand tight, and test the diverter with the shower running.

Shut off the water first if you are working on anything past the spout. The tub often has no stop valves of its own, so the house main or the bath's branch valve may be the only way to do it.

Hiring help for a tub valve in North Ridgeville

Ohio's Department of Commerce gives homeowners a short list when hiring a contractor. Ask for proof of insurance and confirm the policy is active, check references and read a written contract before work starts. Be cautious of anyone who refuses to pull permits or asks for a large payment up front. That list applies to a small valve swap as well as a remodel.

In North Ridgeville, a permit is required when work changes the water supply or the sanitary sewer, and permit rules vary for smaller jobs. Call the Building Division at (440) 490-2081 to ask whether your repair needs one. The city also requires contractors to register, so a plumber who works here should be able to show that.

Households in North Olmsted and Elyria answer to their own building offices, so ask your own city before assuming the same rule applies.

Fix a diverter that lives inside the wall

Wall diverters use a stem, a cartridge or a gate. All three can be replaced without tearing up the tile, because the access is through the handle opening. Pry off the cap, remove the handle screw and the trim plate, and the valve body is behind it.

With the water off, you pull the stem or cartridge and compare it with a replacement. The replacement must match the make and size, and a mismatch causes leaks. Plumbers carry assorted stems for that reason, and a photo of the old part helps you find a match.

If the valve body itself is cracked or the stem will not come out, forcing it can snap a fitting inside the wall. That is the point to stop and call. A seized valve may need to be replaced, which is part of faucet repair work.

Mistakes that turn a small repair into a big one

  • ✓Forcing a seized spout. Twisting hard on a stuck spout can bend the pipe stub inside the wall.
  • ✓Skipping the shutoff. Pulling a stem with the water on sends a jet across the bathroom.
  • ✓Buying a part by guess. Valve stems look alike and differ by size and spline count, so take the old one with you.
  • ✓Over-tightening. A snug fit is enough, and too much force cracks plastic trim or strips threads.

If a part resists after two honest tries, that is the time to stop. A plumber can usually finish the job in a single visit.

When replacing the whole fixture is smarter

If handles are loose, trim is corroded and the valve has been repaired before, a new tub and shower valve may cost less over time than another patch. The same goes for a remodel where the wall is already open.

Replacing the valve is a plumbing installation job, because the water supply connects to the new body and it should be tested before the wall is closed. Plan it with the plumber before tile goes up, not after.

Questions we hear

The diverter is not fully sealing the spout. A worn rubber seal or a mineral-coated stem lets water escape to the tub instead of rising to the shower head.

A spout diverter swap does not need the water off. Anything involving the valve stem or cartridge does. Use the main or the bath's branch valve, then open a faucet to relieve pressure before you start.

A diverter problem shows up as water going to the wrong place. A cartridge problem shows up as wrong temperature, drips from the spout when everything is off, or a handle that does not shut the water completely.

Replacing the spout is the usual repair, because the diverter seal is built into it. Spouts are inexpensive and swap quickly, so a new one is more reliable than trying to patch the old one.

Generally not for a spout. Valve replacements or moved plumbing are different, and permit rules vary, so ask the North Ridgeville Building Division at (440) 490-2081 before you start.

Mineral buildup can weld a handle to its stem. A handle puller and gentle heat usually work. If the handle breaks or the stem turns inside the wall, stop and call a plumber.
