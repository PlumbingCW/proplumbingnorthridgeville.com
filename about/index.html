About North Ridgeville Pro Plumbing

We are a plumbing company based at 34282 Center Ridge Rd in North Ridgeville, Ohio, serving the city and the neighboring towns of Avon, Elyria, Sheffield Village and North Olmsted.

☎ Call (440) 468-0237

What we do

We handle the plumbing problems that cannot wait and the planned work that keeps a house running. That includes drain cleaning, water heater repair, leak detection, sewer line repair and installs. The full list is on the services page.

The phone line is open 24/7. A dispatcher answers, asks what is happening and gives you safe first steps while a plumber is sent.

How we work

A plumber looks at the problem first and gives you a clear price before work starts. If the job turns out to be bigger than it looked, work stops and we explain the change. You decide whether to go ahead.

Where a job needs a city permit, it goes through the North Ridgeville Building Division. North Ridgeville requires contractors to register with the city, and you can confirm that with the Building Division at (440) 490-2081.

How to check any plumber

Ohio does not require a state license for residential plumbing, but commercial plumbing contractors are licensed by the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board. The state suggests asking for proof of insurance, confirming the policy is active, checking references and reading the written contract before work starts. Be cautious of anyone who refuses to pull a permit, pressures you to decide fast, or asks for a large or full payment up front. Complaints can go to the Ohio Attorney General's Consumer Protection Section at 1-800-282-0515.

What a typical call looks like

You call and a person answers. The dispatcher asks what is happening and which fixtures are affected, then gives you steps for water, power or gas. If sewage is involved, you are told to stay out of the affected area. A plumber is sent, finds the cause and explains the options.

The price is given after the plumber has seen the job and before work begins. Nothing is done until you agree. When the work is done, water is run through the fixtures involved to check the result.

Emergency work and planned work

Burst pipes, sewer backups and failed water heaters come first on the phone, and the line is staffed around the clock. Planned work, such as a water heater installation, a sewer camera inspection or repiping, is scheduled with you ahead of time, which also gives you room to ask about permits and options.

The area we know

North Ridgeville has grown from 13,152 residents in 1970 to 35,280 in 2020, so the housing here ranges from older homes to newer subdivisions. Basements are common, winters are hard on pipes, and spring rain tests sump pumps. We also serve Avon, Elyria, Sheffield Village and North Olmsted, and each of those pages explains what is different there.

How prices are set

A fair plumbing price depends on where the problem is, what the repair needs, how easy the work is to reach and whether a permit is involved. A blockage at a sink trap is a short job. A leak under a slab or a break in a buried line takes longer and needs more equipment. That is why we look at the job first and quote after, rather than naming a number over the phone.

Emergency visits at night or on weekends are priced differently from a planned daytime visit, and you are told which applies before the plumber starts. If the scope grows once a wall is open or a camera shows more damage, work pauses while we explain what changed.

What we do not do

We work on plumbing. Foundation cracks, structural repair, mold remediation and electrical work belong to other trades, and we say so when a job points that way. On a wet basement, for example, we handle the sump pump, the floor drains and the pipes, and we tell you plainly when the wall itself needs a different specialist. Our basement waterproofing page spells out where that line sits.

Seasonal plumbing in northern Ohio

Hard freezes put unheated lines at risk, so burst pipe repair calls rise in deep cold. Spring thaw and heavy rain push groundwater toward basements, which is when sump pump repair gets busy. Summer and fall are good times for planned work such as a water heater swap, before the next cold snap.
